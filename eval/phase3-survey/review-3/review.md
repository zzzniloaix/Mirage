### Issue 1 (minor): the PTS test of §4.2's check can't see a reference frame missing at the start of a playlist discontinuity sequence

**Where.** §4.2, the check and what it did (lines 257, 259); §0 item 3 (line 26); Q02 (c) and its Why (lines 758, 763); R11 (line 2026); WP01's test of the check (line 1733) and §9.1's test_timeline_axis.cpp (line 591).

**Issue.** §4.2 says that test (2) tells whether the frames themselves leave the frame grid, a missing frame or a change of rate, so that a run of (1) without one comes from the playlist's EXTINF disagreeing with the frames. Test (2) starts each sequence's grid at the sequence's first scored frame, so when that frame is the one missing, every later PTS of the sequence is on the grid. With the design's own tool and the first frame of each of the ad ladder's three playlist discontinuity sequences removed in turn (frames 0, 75 and 125, simulated), (2) flags nothing, and (1) flags only from the next segment's first frame on: frames 24, 99 and 149 to 198, at −0.040 s (`review-3/runs/seconds-check-sequence-start.txt`). Within the segment that lost its first frame neither test warns, since (c), too, starts that segment at its first remaining frame. The run of (1) without (2) then points the user at the playlist when the frames are what is missing, and the claim of §4.2 and Q02 (c) that (c) is off only where an EXTINF departs from its frames holds here only from the next segment on. A reference that lacks the first frames of a sequence is the case D-057 describes for inputs in general, and R1 lists open GOPs and decoders that output fewer frames than packets as unmeasured. The recommendation still stands: (c) follows the frames again from the next segment, while (d) stays a frame early.

**Suggestion.** Say in §4.2, R11 and Q02 (c) that a frame missing at the start of a playlist discontinuity sequence is seen only by (1), from the next segment on, where it can't be told from an EXTINF one frame too long, and qualify the sentence that attributes a run of (1) alone to the EXTINF accordingly; add the case (a sequence's first frame removed, in the shapes of `review-3/runs/seconds-check-sequence-start.txt`) to WP01's tests and §9.1.

### Issue 5 (minor): §4.2 narrows RFC 8216's rule on whole-second EXTINF

**Where.** §4.2's third bullet (line 254).

**Issue.** §4.2 says RFC 8216 allows whole-second EXTINF only below compatibility version 3, and concludes that whole-second EXTINF are to be expected in playlists of an older version. RFC 8216 §4.3.2.1 (https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8216#section-4.3.2.1) allows a decimal-integer duration at every version: decimal-floating-point is what it recommends, integers are what it requires below version 3, and integer durations are to be rounded to the nearest integer. Whole-second EXTINF are therefore legal in current playlists too, which is the case in which the agent's recommendation makes (d) the better choice. §2 (line 102) and Q02 put it correctly; only §4.2's wording narrows it.

**Suggestion.** Write that RFC 8216 asks for EXTINF accurate enough that their sums don't drift, requires whole seconds below compatibility version 3 and allows them at every version, and drop 'of an older version' from the conclusion.
